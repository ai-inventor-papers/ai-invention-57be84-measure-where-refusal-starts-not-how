
### R12 — Censoring: how many conditions are expected to be lost, and whether the sigmoid stays identifiable. *(the plan's stated contingency (c))*

**The arithmetic first, because it is design-determining and does not depend on any literature.** A 2-parameter logistic (ED50 + slope) has two free parameters, so **at least three distinct dose levels with non-degenerate responses are needed to identify it**, and in practice the data must **bracket** the 50% crossing: if every observed response is on one side of 0.5, ED50 is not point-identified — it is only bounded, and the slope is not identified at all (the likelihood is monotone in it, so the MLE runs to the boundary). On an 8-rung ladder a model that refuses at all 8 rungs yields a **right-censored** ED50 (`> rung 8`) and **no slope**; a model that complies at all 8 yields a **left-censored** ED50 (`< rung 1`) and no slope. These are not noisy estimates to be averaged — they are *missing* estimates, and averaging them as if they were `8` and `1` silently biases every downstream correlation toward zero.

**How many conditions should be expected to censor, from the evidence gathered here.** Three independent sources point the same way and they bracket the problem from both ends.
- **Abliterated arms censor LEFT.** Abliteration is defined by removing refusal, and Hurtado's own registry shows the recipes work: 53/57 uncensored checkpoints are correctly flagged, and the guard labels them uncensored [2]. Expect the abliterated third of every triad to comply at or near every rung.
- **Base arms censor RIGHT or degenerately.** Base models have no chat template and no refusal training; the plan already anticipates them being right-censored, and R3's base-model rule concedes their readings are not template-comparable anyway.
- **Some instruct arms censor LEFT too.** `stabilityai/stablelm-2-1_6b-chat` self-reports *"It is also willing to output potentially harmful outputs or misinformation when the user requests it"* [25], and `SmolLM3`, `MiniCPM`, `InternLM2.5`, `H2O-Danube3` have **no quantified refusal evidence at all** on their cards [28][40][25]. Of the 13 candidate families, only **five** carry a quantified safety number (OLMo-2 87.6 self-reported, Llama-3.1-1B 87.2, Qwen2.5-1.5B 77.6, Gemma-3-1B 70.2, SmolLM2-1.7B 52.4, all on the Tulu-3 *Safety* column of the OLMo-2 card) [24], and **SmolLM2's 52.4 is itself near the middle of that scale** — a family whose instruct model already fails roughly half a safety benchmark is a candidate for left-censoring at the harmful end of the ladder.

So on a 3-arm-per-family design (base / instruct / abliterated), **the plan's worry that censoring "may eat 2/3 of conditions" is the right order of magnitude**: the base and abliterated arms are expected to censor by construction, leaving the instruct arm as the only reliably bracketing condition — and only for the five families with a quantified safety number.

**Design changes.**
1. **Pre-register censoring as an outcome category, not a failure.** Every model-condition gets one of four verdicts: `IDENTIFIED` (crossing bracketed, ED50 and slope both estimated), `RIGHT_CENSORED` (ED50 `>` max dose, slope undefined), `LEFT_CENSORED` (ED50 `<` min dose, slope undefined), `DEGENERATE` (non-monotone or flat). **Report the counts in the primary table.** A design that reports only the identified cells is reporting a survivor-selected subsample.
2. **Use interval-censored estimation, not point estimates plus deletion.** ED50 for a censored condition is an *interval*, and the family-level transfer analysis must consume intervals — a censored-data method (Turnbull NPMLE [50] for the non-parametric crossing, or a hierarchical logistic with partial pooling of the slope across models within family) rather than dropping rows. Partial pooling [52] is the specific remedy for the unidentified slope: it borrows the slope from the family while letting ED50 stay free, which is exactly the right prior given that 2605.05427 reports refusal tendencies are *"stable within model families across generations and scales"* [17].
3. **Add a Firth-penalised fit** [51] as the fallback for near-separated cells, so a condition with 7 refusals and 1 compliance produces a finite slope instead of an infinite one.
4. **Widen the dose range at the benign end before the run, not after.** The continuous anchored axis makes this free: extend `t` below 0 (more benign than the benign anchor) and above 1 (more harmful than the harmful anchor) so that abliterated and weakly-aligned arms still bracket a crossing. This is the single cheapest insurance against the design's main failure mode, and it is impossible to add retroactively.
5. **Power the primary endpoint on IDENTIFIED cells only, and say so.** If only the instruct arms of five families identify, the primary leave-one-family-out analysis has **n = 5 families**, which is the same n as Kim & Han's rotation — adequate for a rotation, far too small for a correlation. State the effective n up front; with 5 families the minimum detectable Spearman ρ is large, so the endpoint must be a *rotation accuracy* (does the held-out family's predicted ordering hold) rather than a correlation coefficient.
