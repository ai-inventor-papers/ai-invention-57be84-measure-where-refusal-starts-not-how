## `disagreement_adjudication`

**Yes — Frank's negative result is about a different object than Kim & Han's positive one, and the hypothesis put to the text survives, with one correction.**

*The correction first.* Kim & Han's transferred object is **not purely a readout axis**. It is a single canonical direction `c_a` deployed **both** as a readout (an ACS coordinate fed to a source-trained probe, or a training-free signed cosine against `c_a`) **and** as an intervention (back-projected via `v_recon = Â^T c_a` and injected into the held-out model's residual stream). On the intervention leg their object is formally the same *kind* of thing Frank tries to move, so a clean "different object" verdict cannot rest on readout-vs-intervention alone.

*What it rests on instead is method and protocol* — and there the two papers are not in contradiction at all. **They are a method and its own no-op control.** Frank performs **zero** cross-model alignment: he drops Qwen3-8B's native political direction unmodified into GLM-4-9B, an operation expressible only because both happen to be 4096-dimensional, and which he reports as literally impossible for DeepSeek and Phi *"because [they] have different hidden dimensions (3584 and 3072 vs. 4096)."* That raw cross-basis copy is **precisely the failure mode Kim & Han's entire contribution exists to repair** — it is what their own framing calls out, that directions *"are defined in a target model's native hidden space and do not directly transfer across model families."* Frank's cosine 0.004 is not evidence against anchor projection; it is a measurement of what happens when you do not project, and Kim & Han would predict a near-null cosine for exactly that operation.

Every other controlled variable differs too. Frank's transfer claim is **n = 1 pair on 48 prompts** (5/48 baseline vs 4/48 transferred, "within noise"); Kim & Han run a **five-rotation leave-one-out over five families**. Frank's object is a **political-censorship routing direction** in Chinese-origin models whose whole point is that it is *mostly orthogonal to the safety direction* (*"orthogonal political/safety representations in three labs (cosine ~0.05)"*); Kim & Han's refusal axis is an English WildJailbreak harmful-vs-benign contrast. Frank's leave-one-out is leave-one-**category**-out *within* a model, never across families.

And Frank's own scope statement concedes the limit. His H3 is that *"routing geometry varies across labs and does not transfer"* — a claim about the **causal gating mechanism**, which he explicitly distinguishes from detection, where he finds the opposite: Yi *"detects political content (probes work) but has orthogonal political-safety geometry and zero behavioral censorship. Detection is present; routing was never installed."*

**Read together the two papers make one coherent claim, not two competing ones:** the detection/readout layer carries shared cross-family structure that an anchor projection can exploit, while the routing layer that converts detection into behaviour is lab-specific and does not survive a raw cross-model copy. Cristofano sits on Frank's side *as to object* — he also moves an intervention — but reports success, and the reason is visible in the method: unlike Frank, he **does** fit a cross-model correspondence (ridge encode → concept basis → decode, DTW layer alignment, weight-SVD guard).

So the field's actual state is **not** "contested about whether transfer works." It is: *settled that naive basis-matched copying fails (Frank), and settled that a fitted cross-model re-expression works for at least some families (Kim & Han, Cristofano), with an explicit known failure family (Gemma).* **The hypothesis's assertion that cross-family transfer of internal safety readouts is an OPEN problem is not merely contested — it is false as written**, and must be rewritten as a scoping claim about *what* is transferred and at what target-side cost.

> **Kim & Han anchor in ACTIVATION space** — their shared coordinate system is a 300-prompt HELM anchor **activation** matrix `Â_m` that every model including the held-out target must be forward-passed to construct, and the object they carry across families is a **vector** (a donor-averaged canonical direction) that must be re-expressed in each new model's basis — **whereas the proposed metric anchors in INPUT space**, defining its dose axis by two fixed English sentences at the prompt, so it builds no cross-model coordinate space, uses no donor model, fits no projection, and carries across families only a **scalar pair (ED50, Hill slope)** plus a metric-to-behaviour mapping that is never re-expressed at all.

## `what_kim_han_already_buy` — stated honestly

They already own **more** than the hypothesis concedes:

1. **Cross-family transfer of a refusal readout with no target-side labels and no fine-tuning.** Per-target refusal binary AUROC **0.94 / 0.95 / 0.97 / 0.93** (Llama/Qwen/Mistral/Phi) → **0.95 ± 0.02** on LQMP, against 0.50 chance.
2. **A leave-one-family-out rotation protocol** — *"a five-rotation evaluation in which each model is held out once"* — structurally the same design as the downstream primary endpoint.
3. **Generalisation to held-out benchmarks without retraining**: *"we evaluate the trained refusal classifier without retraining on JailbreakBench and XSTest. On LQMP, the classifier obtains AUROC 0.81 on JailbreakBench and 0.96 on XSTest."* This is the closest existing thing to a no-recalibration claim.
4. **The cheap-instrument property**: only **75** target forward passes needed — *"Even the smallest pool k=5 (N_anchor=75) reaches 0.832 Multi-CLS / 0.952 Binary-CLS"* — and only **two** donors (L+Q → 0.875/0.954).
5. **The negative half of the story**: a documented family where the geometry breaks — Gemma at **+0.19** mean cosine to LQMP and **0.16** ten-way, *"near chance."*

**What they do NOT buy.** No threshold, no slope, no dose axis, no ED50, no Hill fit, no benign→harmful interpolation. The words *calibration* and *recalibration* **do not appear anywhere in the paper** (regex over full HTML: `recalibrat|calibrat` → *"No matches found"*). Most importantly, **their endpoint is prompt classification, not behaviour prediction**: 0.95 AUROC separates axis-positive from axis-negative *prompts* inside one model; it never predicts a held-out model's behavioural safety *level*, and they report no model-level quantity that could be regressed against behaviour.

> The downstream contribution survives — but only if stated as a claim about predicting **model-level behaviour** from an **input-anchored dose response**, never as a claim that cross-family readout transfer is unsolved.

## Baseline decisions

### `kim_han_is_a_baseline: true`

Decided from the text, against convenience. The text **refutes** the strongest form of the overlap: §3.4 settles that they do *not* need zero target-side computation. But that does not rescue the contribution, because the decisive question is not whether the target-side cost is zero — it is **whether the cost is small enough that the two methods occupy the same operating point**, and Appendix D.1 settles that it is: **75 anchor forward passes already deliver 0.832 / 0.952**. A dose-axis metric interpolating between two anchor sentences spends target forward passes of the same order.

So the downstream paper **cannot differentiate on cost**, **cannot differentiate on the leave-one-family-out design** (they already run it over five families), and **cannot differentiate on freedom from target-side labels** (they already have it). A reviewer will ask the obvious question — *"does an anchor-projected probe, which needs no axis labels on the held-out family either, predict held-out behaviour just as well as your ED50 and slope?"* — and nothing in either paper currently answers it.

**Anchor projection must be RUN, not cited.** Concretely, in its two cheapest forms on the downstream paper's own held-out families: the training-free signed cosine `s_a(x) = ⟨π^pt(a(x)), c_a⟩` and the source-trained logistic probe over ACS coordinates, both at the `k=5` (N=75) pool so compute is matched. Three conditions for a fair comparison:

- **One honest adaptation, to be disclosed not exploited**: Kim & Han emit a *per-prompt* score, so to compete on a per-model endpoint their scores must be aggregated to a model-level statistic (mean score over the harmful set, or harmful-vs-benign AUROC) before regression against behaviour. That adaptation is cheap and fair; refusing to make it would be exactly the convenience this adjudication exists to prevent.
- **Use the Appendix C uniform-fraction layer rule** (f = 5/8, which *"matches uniform f=5/8 within 0.003 binary AUROC"*) rather than the BEST_LAYER dictionary, to avoid importing the layer-selection leak.
- **Include Gemma or a Gemma-like family** in the held-out set — excluding it is what turns their 5-family result into the headline 4-family 0.83/0.95.

### `cristofano_is_a_baseline: false`

Four independent reasons, any one sufficient:

1. **Wrong object and wrong sign.** The output is a rank-one weight edit that *removes* refusal (rates driven from 0.92–0.99 down to 0.00–0.14). Running it as a baseline would measure how effective an ablation *attack* is, not how well a cheap readout predicts behaviour. It emits no score that could be correlated with a held-out family's safety level.
2. **Wrong protocol.** 8 donor→target *pairs* from only two base families, **two of them in-family controls**. No leave-one-family-out rotation, no AUROC, no 0.50 chance baseline.
3. **Incompatible cost and access.** *"strictly white-box"*; on the target alone: ~1000 benign CAR passes at every probed layer, a weight SVD, WikiText drift evaluation, and a **per-target tuned ρ** (Table 1: *"Guard ratio ρ | Per-target | Yes (benign drift)"*). **That per-target tuning is itself a form of recalibration**, making it a poor foil for a no-recalibration claim.
4. **Reproducibility blocker.** Two of eight pairs terminate in unobtainable models — see the GPT-OSS-20B defect below.

**Where he belongs: related work**, and genuinely useful there in two ways — as the *second independent positive result* on the contested side (so "open problem" cannot be rescued by dismissing Kim & Han as a single paper), and as a citable precedent for honest target-side cost accounting (his "Information Budget ℬ₀" and knob-audit table are exactly the bookkeeping device the downstream paper should adopt).

### Absence of evidence — labelled as such

Across **ten queries** I found **no 2026 paper** reporting leave-one-family-out transfer of a cheap safety metric to **behaviour** without recalibration. This is **absence of evidence, not evidence of absence**, and the instruments were weak in a knowable way: **scholarly mode runs on OpenAlex, which returned zero 2026 arXiv preprints across three separate safety queries** and is not a valid index for this literature; general mode found the target papers reliably but is keyword-brittle. The nearest hit, 2607.06596, reports the **opposite**. **Do not upgrade this to a novelty claim.**

## `citation_defects` (10 recorded; the four that will bite hardest)

- **HIGH — 2605.09875, the "+0.46%" figure.** The abs page renders *"refusal-rate shifts of up to +0.46%"*; the HTML body renders the same sentence as *"Δ = +0.46"*, and Table 10 shows an **absolute** refusal-rate change of **0.460** on XSTest — 46 percentage points, not 0.46 percent. Worse, **Δ is the range across the full α sweep, not a shift from baseline**: XSTest canonical reads α=−5: 0.540 … α=0: 0.860 … α=+5: 1.000, so 0.460 = 1.000 − 0.540. Baseline-to-max is only **+0.14**. The **native** direction scores **+0.55** (i.e. *better* than the transfer) and random scores **+0.02**. Never quote "+0.46%".
- **HIGH — 2605.09875, the 0.83/0.95 headline excludes Gemma.** *"excluding Gemma, the trained probes achieve 0.83 mean ten-way accuracy and 0.95 mean binary AUROC."* The fifth family **fails**: 0.16 ten-way ("near chance"), 0.74 AUROC, +0.19 cosine. Cite as **4 of 5 families**, reporting the failure in the same breath.
- **HIGH — 2601.16034, "GPT-OSS-20B".** The abstract advertises *"(including GPT-OSS-20B and GLM-4.6V)"*; §8.1 retracts it: *"Note: 'GPT-OSS-20B' refers to an anonymized internal research model."* Never cite this paper as evidence about OpenAI's gpt-oss-20b.
- **HIGH — 2603.18280, over-citing Frank.** His transfer evidence is **one pair, 48 prompts, no alignment attempted**, with two further models excluded for dimension mismatch, and his scoped claim is about **routing**: *"H3 (routing geometry varies across labs and does not transfer)."* He separately reports detection **generalises** (LOCO-CV 87.5–100%). Cite as *"raw cross-basis direction transfer of a routing direction fails (n=1 pair, cosine 0.004)"*, **never** as *"cross-family transfer of safety readouts fails."*

Also recorded: the Kim & Han layer-selection leak (§4.2's criterion needs the held-out target's own native direction — **mitigated** by Appendix C's uniform f=5/8 matching BEST_LAYER within 0.003 AUROC); Cristofano's thin family coverage (2 base families, 2 of 8 pairs in-family, two VLM targets) and his unconfirmed "ICML" keyword; the brief's mischaracterisation of Frank's nine models as "Chinese-language" (one is Microsoft's Phi-4-mini; they are Chinese-**origin** models tested on political censorship); the zero occurrences of "calibrat*" in Kim & Han; and the falsity of the hypothesis's "open problem" framing.
