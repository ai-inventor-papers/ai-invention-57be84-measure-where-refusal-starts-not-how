
### R13 — The first-token readout reads exactly the layer the field calls "shallow", and it is a lossy proxy even there. **This is the most dangerous single finding in this artifact.**
Four independent papers converge on it:
- **Qi et al., 2406.05946, *"Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep"*** [29]: *"safety alignment can take shortcuts, wherein the alignment adapts a model's generative distribution primarily over only its very first few output tokens. We refer to this issue as shallow safety alignment."* The quantity our screen reads **is** the shortcut the field is trying to eliminate.
- **Kwon, 2607.14147** [22], graded as FATAL by the adversarial sweep: *"restoring the harm signal over the first half of the response recovers refusal as much as the whole response, while the second half recovers almost nothing and **the onset token alone only 9%**."* So even *inside* the shallow regime, the single onset token carries roughly **9% of the causal weight** that the first half of the response carries (42% vs 41% for the whole response, against a 3% baseline). A first-token read is a lossy proxy for the very thing it is proxying.
- **Yang et al., 2609.18471, *"First Token Matters: Understanding Safety Collapse in Large Reasoning Models"*** [30]: *"while the refusal projection strength of LRMs remains aligned with that of IT models during the prompt encoding phase, it drops sharply at the first generated token... we term Onset Refusal Collapse (ORC)."* The signal collapses **specifically at the token we read**, and specifically for reasoning models — i.e. exactly the Qwen3/SmolLM3 class.
- **Park et al., 2605.29629, *"Beyond Attack Success Rate: Temporal Logit Observability for LLM Safety Failures"*** [31]: *"The same ASR-success label can hide three fundamentally different processes: refusal evidence may have appeared briefly and been suppressed; refusal may have emerged but too late to matter; or refusal may never have been triggered at all."*

**The counter-evidence, stated fairly.** The Logit-Gap paper's own Position-1 decision census finds the first generated token lands in the refusal set **96.0–98.8%** of the time under greedy decoding across three families on 520 AdvBench prompts [9]. So the first token is a *reliable indicator of the eventual behaviour on clean harmful prompts*, even if it is a *weak causal handle*. Those two facts are compatible, and the distinction is the honest framing: **anchoring calibrates the SCALE of the readout, not its DEPTH.** The proposal's "anchored levels transfer, unanchored levels don't" framing does not address the depth threat at all.

**Design changes.** (i) **Validate, never substitute.** Report the first-token ED50 alongside a full-generation judged outcome on a subset, and report the attenuation explicitly as a number. (ii) Add a **first-half-of-response window** readout as a secondary condition, since that is where the causal weight demonstrably lives [22]. (iii) **Stress-test the reasoning families separately** (Qwen3, SmolLM3) against ORC [30] rather than assuming `enable_thinking=False` fully repairs them — the ORC result says the collapse is at the first *generated* token, which is what we read even after the empty think-pair. (iv) Pre-register that a favourable (ED50, slope) **does not** license any claim of robustness to prefill or suffix attacks, which are documented to defeat first-few-token safety signals specifically [29][22].

### R14 — Dose-level count: the 2-point optimum is a trap, and ~15 forward passes is not obviously enough.
Two citable benchmarks exist and they disagree, which is itself the finding [32]. The classical **locally D-optimal** design for a 2-parameter logistic concentrates all weight at exactly **two** dose points (standardized logit ±1.5434) — but it is a *local* optimum that **requires already knowing the true threshold and slope to place the points**, so it is fragile precisely when they are unknown. Practical robust dose-response design guidance goes the other way: *"requiring duplicates would, after rounding, work out to 28 total observations needed, 2 each for the 8 central doses, plus 6 each for the minimum and maximum doses... The resulting design places observations on the two shoulders of the curve, where a small movement in dose changes the response probability, rather than where the curve is flat."*
**Design change.** Justify the rung count and spacing **explicitly in the paper**, and do not lean on the 2-point theoretical minimum. Use roughly **8–10 central levels plus boundary levels**, with the boundary levels replicated more heavily — the continuous anchored axis makes the levels free to choose, so the only real cost is forward passes, and a ~15-pass budget should be spent as ~10 levels with heavier sampling at the two shoulders rather than as 15 evenly spaced points. Combined with R12's advice to extend `t` beyond [0,1], this is the design's single best return on effort.

### R15 — Cross-family calibration transfer fails by a *measured* amount elsewhere. That is now the bar.
Two matched negative results give a concrete number the claim must beat [33][34]:
- **2607.13346, *"The Refusal Residue: When Probes Catch Alignment Faking and When They Don't"***: *"Under leakage-free leave-one-query-out probing, detection survives on Llama (AUROC 0.87) but collapses to chance on Qwen (0.43), and cross-model transfer is null."* The paper also reports cross-model Procrustes alignment is null against a random-Procrustes baseline.
- **2608.02464**: *"monitors do not transfer across deployments without recalibration: calibrated on qwen2.5:7b and deployed on llama3.1:8b over a matched task, tool and injector plan, the primary monitor sits at chance (AUROC 0.527, healthy FA 0.75), while the same target recalibrated on itself reaches 0.885."*
- A general structural caution, **2606.03280**: *"Replacement-style injection is consistently destructive, and rescaling translated vectors to the receiver hidden-state norm does not rescue performance... offline representational alignment is not sufficient for useful causal communication inside the receiver"* — high cross-model representational similarity (normalised cosine ≈0.97) does **not** imply causal usability after transfer [35].
**Design change.** Set the bar numerically and publicly: the paper must show that anchoring closes a gap of the size **0.87 → 0.43** or **0.885 → 0.527**. Report **per-family calibration curves**, not only a pooled correlation, so a reviewer can see whether the gap actually closed. Include a **random-Procrustes-style negative control** (the 2607.13346 and 2607.06596 rotation controls are the same idea) so a null is distinguishable from a method.

### R16 — Three live competitors occupy adjacent ground, one of them strongly. **Position against them in related work or the paper will read as unaware.**
The exact recipe (two fixed anchor sentences → first-token logit → Hill ED50/slope, no parent, no labels, no generations, ~15 passes) **was not found verbatim anywhere searched** — reported as "not found", not as novelty. But the surrounding space is not empty:
- **ProbGuard, 2608.10621, *"Calibrated Safety Risk Estimation from LLM Output Distributions"*** [36] — **the most dangerous**. It claims to be *"the first completely probabilistic architecture-agnostic guardrail"*, estimating a calibrated probability that the continuation will be unsafe from the first ~10 decoding steps' output distributions, **without hidden states**, and reports reducing average Brier score and ECE by **79.6% and 71.9%** over the best baseline **across three LLM families and three safety datasets**, limiting attack success rate to ≤1% across six jailbreak attacks. That is an already-published, stronger, validated cross-family generalisation result from early-decoding logits than the proposal has yet produced. **It needs no parent model.**
- **SelfGrader, 2604.01473, *"LLM Jailbreak Detection via Anchored Token-Level Logits"*** [37] — the name is nearly our method's name. It reads a harmfulness score directly off the logits of numeric tokens (0–9), calibrated to a rubric via PAC-guided in-context **anchor** examples, one forward pass, no generation, no parent. Its object is a **per-query** score rather than a **model-level** (ED50, slope), which is the distinction our paper must draw explicitly and in the same sentence it claims "anchored".
- **Fast Proxies for LLM Robustness Evaluation, 2502.10487** [38] — a cheap single-greedy-generation "direct ASR" proxy that predicts full red-team-ensemble ASR at **Spearman rs = 0.94, Pearson rp = 0.87**, validated **across** Gemma2 / Mistral / Qwen / Phi3 / Llama3 / Llama2, no parent model, ~300 prompts, *"three orders of magnitude"* cheaper than the ensemble it predicts. **This is the honest cheap-baseline bar for "few prompts, good safety estimate":** it already works cross-family at rs = 0.94. It needs generations and a judge, which is our differentiator — but the differentiator is *cost and access*, not *ability*.
- **escapement (escapements.org)** [39] — a live deployed register producing an injection-compliance rate with 95% CI and a letter grade per checkpoint, framed as a **paired delta against the checkpoint's own base/parent** (e.g. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-Uncensored at 36.7% vs 18.5% for its base), from 40–173 generation trials per model. Parent-dependent and generation-dependent, so it sits on the far side of our requirement profile — but it is what a platform actually runs today, and a paper proposing a screen for platforms should cite it.

**Design change.** Add all four to the competing-deliverables table and to related work, and state the requirement-profile differentiator in one sentence each. Specifically: against ProbGuard, our claim must be *model-level threshold-and-slope with zero generations and no calibration set*, not *cross-family calibration*, which ProbGuard already has. Against SelfGrader, the claim must be *model-level, not per-query*. Against Fast Proxies, the claim must be *no generations and no judge*, since it already beats us on demonstrated cross-family rank correlation.

### R17 — The censoring gap is genuinely unreported in the LLM literature, which is an opportunity.
The adversarial sweep searched directly for any LLM paper that fits a dose-response/Hill/logistic curve to graded harmful prompts and reports the fraction of conditions that censor, and **found none** — the problem is well documented in psychometrics and pharmacology but has not been quantified for LLM safety dose-response curves. This is absence of evidence, labelled as such.
**Design change.** **Report the censored fraction as a first-class result.** If nobody has published it, a clean table of "how many model-conditions on a graded harm ladder admit an identified 50% crossing, broken down by base / instruct / abliterated and by family" is a contribution in its own right, and it is free — it falls out of the run whether the primary endpoint succeeds or fails. Pre-register it so it is reported either way.
