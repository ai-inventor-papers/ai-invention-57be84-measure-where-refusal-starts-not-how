
### R10 — A CPU-feasible behavioural oracle exists, and it is the same one the competitor used.
Hurtado's ground-truth labels come from *"A behavioral oracle (Qwen3Guard (Qwen Team, 2025), think-traces stripped)"* [2]. That model ships at **0.6B** — `Qwen/Qwen3Guard-Gen-0.6B` and `Qwen/Qwen3Guard-Stream-0.6B`, with an **ONNX-quantized** community build (`rogerdeng/Qwen3Guard-0.6B-ONNX-Quantized`, `nxp/Qwen3Guard-Gen-0.6B-ONNX`) [27][28]. A 0.6B ONNX classifier is runnable on the 2-CPU / 20 GB budget.
**Design change (an opportunity, not a hazard).** Use Qwen3Guard-Gen-0.6B as the *in-house* behavioural oracle so the comparison against 2607.01854 is **oracle-matched** rather than oracle-confounded, and keep the two-judge StrongREJECT ensemble (R6) as the independent check on the oracle itself. Being able to state "we used the same behavioural oracle as the parent-dependent competitor, and still beat/matched its held-out-family number without a parent" is a materially stronger claim than beating it under a different oracle. Strip think-traces exactly as Hurtado does, and cite the technical report (arXiv:2510.14276).

Two further facts that make this the right choice. **Licence: apache-2.0** [27] — no gating, no redistribution restriction, unlike SORRY-Bench. And it emits a **three-level** label, not a binary: the model card's own parser is `r"Safety: (Safe|Unsafe|Controversial)"` over ten categories (`Violent | Non-violent Illegal Acts | Sexual Content or Sexual Acts | PII | Suicide & Self-Harm | Unethical Acts | Politically Sensitive Topics | Copyright Violation | Jailbreak | None`) [27]. **The `Controversial` middle tier is an externally-authored ordinal middle rung**, structurally parallel to OR-Bench's `hard-1k`, and gives a third independent ordinal check on the dose axis at zero extra cost — the ED50 should fall near the Safe→Controversial→Unsafe transition if the axis measures what it claims to.

### R11 — "Dose-matched" is already used in this literature in a DIFFERENT sense. Do not collide with it.
2607.14147 uses *"a dose-matched position control"* to mean an intervention band of **matched width at matched strength**, not a graded harm axis: *"Restoring the harm direction over a dose-matched band of the response under the attack (n = 100, Qwen2.5-1.5B, L12/α=1): the first half restores refusal to 42%, as much as the whole response (41%), while the second half restores only 6% at double strength and the onset alone 9%"*, against a 3% no-injection baseline [22]. Code and ledger at `https://github.com/collapseindex/breaking-refusal`.
**Design change (terminological, but it will cost a reviewer's goodwill if missed).** Name our axis unambiguously — *harm-dose axis* or *anchor-interpolation axis* — and, when citing 2607.14147, say explicitly that its "dose" is intervention strength/width while ours is prompt-level harm. Better still, **borrow its control design**: its equal-attention-mass and equal-band-width controls are exactly the "matched-magnitude placebo" a reviewer will demand of a graded-dose claim, and it publishes a reproducible ledger we can mirror.
