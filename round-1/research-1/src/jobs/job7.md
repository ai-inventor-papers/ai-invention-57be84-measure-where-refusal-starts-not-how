# Job 7 -- Adversarial Cross-Check of the Anchored Dose-Axis Safety Metric

Attacking the proposal on four fronts: (A) does first-token logit readout predict downstream behaviour, (B) does the dose-response fit survive censoring, (C) is cross-family transfer already a documented negative result, (D) does a cheap arbitrary-HF-model safety score already exist. Full data with every quote/URL is in `job7.json`.

## A. First-token logit readout as a predictor of downstream behaviour

**Verdict: strong, unresolved threat.** The exact quantity the proposal reads -- the logit at the first assistant token -- is independently and mechanistically identified, across four separate 2026-era papers, as precisely the locus of known alignment failure, not a neutral behavioral proxy.

The central paper is confirmed: **Qi et al., "Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep," arXiv:2406.05946 (ICLR 2025)**. Verified via `arxiv.org/abs/2406.05946`: authors Xiangyu Qi, Ashwinee Panda, Kaifeng Lyu, Xiao Ma, Subhrajit Roy, Ahmad Beirami, Prateek Mittal, Peter Henderson. Its core claim, quoted verbatim: "safety alignment can take shortcuts, wherein the alignment adapts a model's generative distribution primarily over only its very first few output tokens. We refer to this issue as shallow safety alignment." The paper's own remedy is to *deepen* safety past the first tokens -- the opposite direction from reading only token one.

A sharper, more recent mechanistic paper puts a number on how little the single onset token actually carries: **Kwon, "Breaking Refusal in the First Half: A Mechanistic Study of the Prefill Jailbreak," arXiv:2607.14147**. Quote: "restoring the harm signal over the first half of the response recovers refusal as much as the whole response, while the second half recovers almost nothing and the onset token alone only 9%." That is, within the causal window that *does* carry the refusal decision, the single first token accounts for roughly a fifth of the effect of the first half of the response (9% vs 42%). A first-token-only readout is therefore a lossy compression even of the "shallow" signal it is trying to measure.

**Beyond Attack Success Rate: Temporal Logit Observability for LLM Safety Failures**, arXiv:2605.29629 (Park et al.), makes the identifiability problem explicit: "The same ASR-success label can hide three fundamentally different processes: refusal evidence may have appeared briefly and been suppressed; refusal may have emerged but too late to matter; or refusal may never have been triggered at all." A single first-token snapshot cannot tell these apart, so two models with the same (ED50, slope) reading could have arrived there through qualitatively different, and differently-robust, mechanisms.

Finally, **"First Token Matters: Understanding Safety Collapse in Large Reasoning Models," arXiv:2609.18471**, shows the failure is architecture-specific in a way that directly threatens reasoning models: "while the refusal projection strength of LRMs remains aligned with that of IT models during the prompt encoding phase, it drops sharply at the first generated token" (their "Onset Refusal Collapse"). This corroborates the memory note that Qwen3-style thinking blocks break first-token readout -- it is not an implementation quirk, it is a documented phenomenon.

**Most dangerous single finding for A:** Kwon 2607.14147's 9%-vs-42% number -- the onset token is a measurably weak causal handle even within the population of models everyone agrees is "shallowly aligned."

## B. Does the dose-response / Hill fit survive censoring?

Searched extensively for an LLM paper that fits dose-response/Hill curves to graded harmful prompts and reports the censored fraction; **found none** -- this appears to be a genuine, unaddressed gap, not something the proposal can point to as "solved elsewhere." (Queries run: ED50/censored dose-response, ceiling/floor IRT ability estimation, min dose levels for a 2-parameter logistic.)

The general statistics/psychometrics literature does supply concrete, citable remedies:

- **Turnbull's (1976) NPMLE / self-consistency algorithm** for interval- and right-censored data. Quote (via a worked explainer): "it is known for the individuals that are right censored that the event of interest did not occur until the last visit but it can happen at any time from that moment on" -- exactly the "always refuses at every rung" case. Source: `ms.uky.edu/~mai/splus/IcensEM.pdf`.
- **Firth's (1993) penalized likelihood**, the standard fix when MLE does not exist because of separation: "penalized likelihood also has the attraction of producing finite, consistent estimates of regression parameters when the maximum likelihood estimates do not even exist because of complete or quasi-complete separation." Source: `statisticalhorizons.com/logistic-regression-for-rare-events/`.
- **Hierarchical Bayesian partial pooling** across models/families, which shrinks an extreme (all-0 or all-1) group's estimate toward the population mean rather than leaving it undefined. Source: the `rstanarm` partial-pooling vignette (Carpenter, Gabry, Goodrich).

On the **minimum number of dose levels**: no single number is settled, and neither citable benchmark maps cleanly onto a 15-forward-pass, 2-anchor design. The classical *locally D-optimal* design for a 2-parameter logistic model puts all its weight on exactly **2** points (at standardized logit ±1.5434) -- but this is a *local* optimum that requires already knowing the true threshold and slope to place those 2 points correctly, which is circular for a method whose whole point is to estimate threshold and slope from scratch. The practical, *robust* design literature (Kappenberg et al., PMC13221392) instead recommends spreading many more levels with replication -- one worked design used 8 central dose levels plus 2 boundary levels, "28 total observations needed, 2 each for the 8 central doses, plus 6 each for the minimum and maximum doses," specifically to be robust to not knowing the parameters in advance.

**Most dangerous single finding for B:** the field-standard robust design for exactly this problem (unknown threshold+slope, 2-parameter logistic) uses roughly 8-10 dose levels with replication, not the 2 the proposal seems to imply are sufficient -- and no one has yet measured, for LLM safety specifically, how often the crossing simply doesn't exist.

## C. Cross-family calibration transfer as a known negative result

This is where the proposal is most directly threatened by a strong existing quantitative negative result. **"The Refusal Residue: When Probes Catch Alignment Faking and When They Don't," arXiv:2607.13346**: "Under leakage-free leave-one-query-out probing, detection survives on Llama (AUROC 0.87) but collapses to chance on Qwen (0.43), and cross-model transfer is null." That is a 0.87-to-0.43 collapse for a hidden-state safety-relevant probe going from one family to another, with cross-model Procrustes alignment separately reported as "null under a random-Procrustes baseline."

Even more directly relevant is **"Real-Time Detection and Repair of LLM Agent Failures," arXiv:2608.02464**, which states as a structural finding: "monitors do not transfer across deployments without recalibration: calibrated on qwen2.5:7b and deployed on llama3.1:8b over a matched task, tool and injector plan, the primary monitor sits at chance (AUROC 0.527, healthy FA 0.75), while the same target recalibrated on itself reaches 0.885." This is the precise failure mode -- a safety-adjacent monitor calibrated on one family and deployed unchanged on another -- that the proposal's headline claim ("transfers leave-one-family-out with NO recalibration") must beat. The gap the proposal needs to close (0.527 -> something well above chance) is exactly the gap this paper measures as the default outcome of NOT anchoring/recalibrating.

A third, more general caution: **"A Negative Result on Cross-Model Activation Transfer in a Pythia Multi-Hop Setting," arXiv:2606.03280** shows that even very high cross-model representational similarity (normalized cosine ~0.97) does not guarantee the representation is causally usable after transfer -- "offline representational alignment is not sufficient for useful causal communication inside the receiver." This is a structural warning against assuming similarity implies transferability, independent of whether anchoring helps.

**Most dangerous single finding for C:** the 0.885 (same-model) vs 0.527 (chance, cross-family, no recalibration) numbers in 2608.02464 -- this is a real, recent, precisely-matched demonstration of the exact failure mode the proposal claims anchoring solves, giving reviewers a concrete number to demand the proposal beat.

## D. Does a cheap few-prompt safety score for arbitrary HF models already exist?

The proposal's *exact* pipeline (2 fixed anchor sentences, first-token logit only, no generations, no parent, ~15 forward passes, cross-family Hill-curve transfer) was not found verbatim anywhere searched. But the surrounding design space is populated by several close, numerically-validated competitors, and this should be stated plainly:

- **escapement (escapements.org)** is a live, deployed product doing "independent safety evaluation for AI checkpoints" on real Hugging Face models, reporting an injection-compliance rate with 95% CI (e.g. "18.46% [10.9, 29.6]") from 40-173 generation trials per model. It is a genuine "cheap-ish safety score for arbitrary open-weight models" service, but it needs actual generations and a paired parent/base model (opposite design point from the proposal on both axes).
- **"Fast Proxies for LLM Robustness Evaluation," arXiv:2502.10487**, is the closest match on the "cheap + cross-family + no parent" axes: a single greedy generation plus judge per prompt ("direct ASR") predicts full expensive red-team-ensemble attack success rate at "rs=0.94 (Spearman), rp=0.87 (Pearson)," explicitly validated **across** model families (Gemma2, Mistral, Qwen, Phi3, Llama3, Llama2), at "three orders of magnitude" lower cost. It needs one generation per prompt rather than a bare logit, but it already occupies most of the value proposition.
- **"SelfGrader: LLM Jailbreak Detection via Anchored Token-Level Logits," arXiv:2604.01473**, is the closest match on framing: it explicitly does "anchored token-level logit" safety scoring, using PAC-guided in-context "anchor examples" to calibrate a numeric-token logit scale into "a stable and interpretable score that reflects harmfulness." It operates per-query (jailbreak detection) rather than fitting a cross-family dose-response curve, and needs an ICL anchor prompt rather than two fixed sentences, but the phrase "anchored ... logits" and the calibration mechanism are strikingly close to the proposal's own framing.
- **"ProbGuard: Calibrated Safety Risk Estimation from LLM Output Distributions," arXiv:2608.10621**, explicitly claims to be "the first completely probabilistic architecture-agnostic guardrail," reading only the first ~10 decoding steps' output distributions (a window, not a single token) and reporting "reducing the average Brier score and ECE by 79.6% and 71.9%, respectively, over the best baseline" across three LLM families and three datasets, with attack success rate limited to at most 1% across six jailbreak attacks. This is a stronger, already-published, numerically-validated cross-family generalization result than the proposal has yet produced, on the specific axis ("logit-based signal generalizes across architectures without per-family retraining") the proposal is claiming as novel.

**Verdict, stated plainly:** nothing found does the proposal's precise construction (fixed 2-sentence anchor, single first-token read, Hill ED50/slope, no generations, no parent, no recalibration). But each of the proposal's three selling points -- anchoring against fixed references, cheapness (no generations / near-zero forward passes), and cross-family generalization -- already has a separate, published, numerically-validated existence proof elsewhere (SelfGrader for anchoring, Fast Proxies for cross-family cheapness, ProbGuard for cross-family generalization with large reported gains). The proposal's contribution would need to be the *combination*, explicitly positioned against all three, not simply "no one has done this."

**Most dangerous single finding for D:** ProbGuard's reported 79.6%/71.9% calibration-error reduction and architecture-agnostic claim is a stronger, already-published cross-family generalization result on a logit-based safety signal than anything demonstrated yet for the proposal.

## Design changes implied

1. Add an explicit per-condition censoring detector (flag all-refuse / all-comply at every rung) and report the censored fraction by family/model; use a Turnbull-style interval-censored NPMLE, or report a one-sided bound, instead of force-fitting a point ED50 when censored.
2. Fit the Hill curve with Firth-penalized (bias-reduced) logistic likelihood, or a hierarchical/partially-pooled Bayesian model across families, rather than plain MLE, given the small number of dose rungs (~15 forward passes) and the near-certainty of quasi-separation at some rungs.
3. Justify the number and spacing of dose levels: the 2-point locally-optimal design assumes the threshold/slope are already known, which they are not here; the practical dose-response design literature recommends ~8-10 levels with replication instead.
4. Do not treat the first-assistant-token logit as ground truth on its own -- validate it against full-generation judged outcomes and against a first-half-of-response window; report the attenuation (the onset token was shown elsewhere to carry only ~9% of the causal effect the first half of the response carries).
5. Explicitly exclude or separately stress-test reasoning/chain-of-thought models -- the first-generated-token refusal signal is independently reported to collapse specifically at the reasoning-onset token for LRMs.
6. Pre-register a check against self-correction / late-refusal / suppressed-then-recovered-refusal failure modes so a favorable (ED50, slope) reading is not mistaken for robustness to prefill or adversarial-suffix attacks, which are documented to defeat exactly first-few-token safety signals.
7. Set the "transfers without recalibration" bar against the documented size of calibration-transfer failure elsewhere (0.87->0.43; 0.885->0.527) and report per-family calibration curves, not only a pooled correlation.
8. Add SelfGrader, ProbGuard, and Fast Proxies for LLM Robustness Evaluation to related work and state precisely what differs from each, since each already has a numerically validated claim on one of the proposal's three central selling points.
